How a phone finds one song so fast

Your phone never checks every song: it reads a few signs, then a few more, like walking a big shop.

That is why search feels instant, even when the library is huge.

Too many shelves

You walk into a huge music shop. You want one song. Checking every shelf would take all day.

Signs over the aisles

So the shop hangs signs over each aisle: rock, jazz, pop. You read three signs, not every cover.

Go deeper

With M songs and groups of size ∛M, you read about 3·∛M signs: λ = 0.72, KBLaM, HiKVP, K3.

Signs inside the aisle

Inside the jazz aisle, smaller signs split it again. Each sign stands for a whole group of shelves. You keep the best match and walk on.

Why it feels instant

Two rounds of signs take you from the whole shop to about 300 covers. That is why the search feels instant.

The catch: if the first sign points the wrong way, you never see the right shelf.
